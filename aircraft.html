The challenge

Remote communities north of the Arctic Circle depend on aircraft for people, supplies, and medical transport. The team explored a smaller, affordable aircraft capable of operating from constrained runways while accommodating harsh-weather navigation and operational demands.

The engineering approach

I developed the MATLAB code, performed the engineering calculations, and built the computational models used for the team’s aircraft sizing and performance analysis. Starting from stakeholder needs and requirements, the team evaluated existing aircraft and competing configurations, estimated gross takeoff weight, selected wing and tail geometry, analyzed drag, and modeled takeoff, landing, power, and climb performance. The design selected a high-wing, fixed-gear configuration and a Pratt & Whitney PT6A-140 turboprop.

My technical contributions

I was responsible for the project’s MATLAB scripts, numerical calculations, and modeling. My work covered iterative aircraft weight and fuel sizing, wing geometry and drag-polar calculations, takeoff and landing estimates, and performance models for power required versus velocity and rate of climb. The resulting analyses informed the team’s configuration and engine decisions.

Design decisions & tradeoffs

The report considers wing and airfoil characteristics, icing mitigation, landing gear configuration, avionics, cargo access, and aircraft performance. These decisions were evaluated against the team's mission requirements and operational constraints.

What the work demonstrates

Hands-on MATLAB development, numerical modeling, requirements-driven engineering, preliminary sizing, quantitative performance estimates, multidisciplinary design tradeoffs, and technical communication through a comprehensive report.

This was a student team conceptual design, not a manufactured or flight-tested aircraft. Performance figures are estimates reported by the team, not independently validated test results.
